## **Who is an Adversary?**

**adversary** --> attacker

They use tools, skills, and techniques (**capabilities**) to achieve a specific **intent**, such as stealing data, spying, or causing damage.


- **Adversary Operator:** The hacker or individuals who actually perform the attack or intrusion.
    
- **Adversary Customer:** The person, organization, or group that benefits from the attack. They may hire or control the operators.


A **victim** is the target of a cyberattack. It can be a **person, organization, email address, IP address, domain, system, or network** that the adversary wants to attack.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">There are two main parts of a victim</mark>:

- **Victim Personae:** The people or organizations being targeted (e.g., company employees, managers, specific industries, or individuals).
    
- **Victim Assets:** The technical resources that can be attacked, such as **computers, networks, email accounts, IP addresses, domains, or social media accounts**.


**Capability** refers to the **skills, tools, and techniques** an adversary uses to carry out a cyberattack. It represents the attacker’s **Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs)**.

Capabilities can range from **simple methods** like manual password guessing to **advanced methods** such as creating malware or malicious tools.

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">Key related terms:</mark>

- **Capability Capacity:** The vulnerabilities or weaknesses that a specific capability can exploit.
    
- **Adversary Arsenal:** The full collection of capabilities an adversary has. It includes all their tools, malware, and attack techniques.

**Infrastructure** refers to the **software, hardware, and network resources** that an adversary uses to deliver attacks or control compromised systems. Examples include **IP addresses, domain names, email accounts, command-and-control (C2) servers, or malicious USB devices**.

Types of infrastructure:

- **Type 1 Infrastructure:** Directly **owned or controlled by the adversary**.
    
- **Type 2 Infrastructure:** **Controlled by an intermediary** (sometimes unknowingly). It hides the real attacker and makes attribution harder. Examples include **compromised servers, malicious domains, and hacked email accounts**.

**Service Providers** are organizations that support this infrastructure, such as **Internet Service Providers (ISPs), domain registrars, and webmail providers**. They help keep the adversary’s infrastructure running.
