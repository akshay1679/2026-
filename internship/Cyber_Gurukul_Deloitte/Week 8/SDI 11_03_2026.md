**MISP Feed – Short Version (Key Points)**

**1. What is a Feed?**

- A **feed is an external threat intelligence data source** connected to MISP.
    
- It automatically provides **Indicators of Compromise (IoCs)** like malicious IPs, domains, hashes, and URLs.
    

**2. Why We Use Feeds in MISP**

- To **automatically import threat intelligence** from trusted sources.
    
- To keep the system **updated with the latest cyber threats**.
    
- To **reduce manual work** of adding threat indicators.
    

**3. What Feeds Contain**  
Feeds usually include:

- Malicious **IP addresses**
    
- Malicious **domains**
    
- **File hashes** (malware)
    
- **Phishing URLs**
    
- **Botnet servers**
    

**4. What Happens When a Feed is Enabled**

1. MISP connects to the **feed URL**
    
2. Downloads threat intelligence data
    
3. Stores it in **feed cache**
    
4. Converts it into **MISP events and attributes**
    

**5. Purpose of Feeds**

- Improve **threat detection**
    
- Enable **threat intelligence sharing**
    
- Help security teams **identify attacks faster**
    

**6. Example Feeds (from your screenshot)**

- **CIRCL OSINT Feed**
    
- **Botvrij.eu Data**
    

These provide **open-source threat intelligence (OSINT)**.

**7. “Not Cached” Status**

- Means the feed is **enabled but data is not downloaded yet**.
    
- You must click **Cache all feeds** or **Fetch and store feed data**.
    

✅ **In simple words:**  
_A MISP feed automatically imports threat intelligence from external sources into your MISP server._
