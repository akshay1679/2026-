A **supply chain attack** is when hackers don’t attack _you_ directly — instead, they break into **someone you trust** and use that access to reach you.

### Simple idea

> _If an attacker can’t get in through the front door, they sneak in through a trusted delivery._

---

### Simple example (very easy to picture)

Imagine this situation 👇

![Image](https://marvel-b1-cdn.bc0a.com/f00000000310757/www.fortinet.com/content/dam/fortinet/images/cyberglossary/what-is-malware.png)

![Image](https://www.netsuite.com/portal/assets/img/business-articles/inventory-management/infographic-supply-chain-attack.jpg)

![Image](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/375837353/figure/fig1/AS%3A11431281206501875%401700732074686/Typical-flow-chart-for-a-secure-software-update-procedure-of-non-volatile-memory.png)

1. You install a popular app on your computer.
    
2. The app regularly sends you **official updates**.
    
3. Hackers secretly break into the **company that makes the app**.
    
4. They insert **malicious code** into a normal-looking update.
    
5. You install the update, trusting it.
    
6. Boom — malware is now on your computer.
    

👉 You did **nothing wrong**. The attack came through a **trusted supplier**.

---

### Another everyday analogy (non-technical)

Think of a **restaurant** 🍽️

- You trust the restaurant.
    
- The restaurant trusts its food supplier.
    
- A hacker poisons the food **before it reaches the restaurant**.
    
- Customers get sick — even though the restaurant followed the rules.
    

That’s a **supply chain attack**.

---

### Why supply chain attacks are dangerous

- They hit **many victims at once**
    
- They abuse **trust**
    
- They’re hard to detect
    
- Even security-conscious users can be affected
    

---

### Famous real-world example (optional knowledge)

In 2020, hackers compromised a company called **SolarWinds** and spread malware through its software updates to thousands of organizations.

---

### One-line definition

> A **supply chain attack** is when attackers compromise a trusted vendor or supplier to secretly attack their customers.
