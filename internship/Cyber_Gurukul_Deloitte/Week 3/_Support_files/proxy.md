## What is a proxy?

A **proxy** is an **intermediary** that sits between you and something else, passing requests and responses back and forth on your behalf.

Think of it like a **middleman**.

### In everyday terms

Instead of:

> **You → Website**

It becomes:

> **You → Proxy → Website → Proxy → You**

### What proxies are commonly used for

- **Privacy:** Hides your real IP address from websites
    
- **Security:** Filters malicious traffic or blocks unsafe sites
    
- **Access control:** Lets organizations restrict or monitor internet use
    
- **Speed:** Can cache (store) content so it loads faster next time
    
- **Bypassing restrictions:** Access content blocked in certain regions or networks
    

### Common types of proxies

- **Web proxy:** Accessed via a browser, no setup needed
    
- **Forward proxy:** Used by users to access the internet
    
- **Reverse proxy:** Used by servers to manage incoming traffic
    
- **Transparent proxy:** You don’t even know it’s there (often used by schools or workplaces)
    

### Simple analogy

It’s like asking a friend to go order food for you:

- The restaurant sees **your friend**, not you
    
- Your friend brings the food back to you
    
- You stay a little more anonymous 😄
