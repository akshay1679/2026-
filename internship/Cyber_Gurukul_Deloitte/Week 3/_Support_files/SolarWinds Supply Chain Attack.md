The SolarWinds attack (often called **Sunburst**) was a massive, highly sophisticated state-sponsored cyber espionage campaign discovered in December 2020. It is considered one of the most successful "supply chain attacks" in history because it didn't target the victims directly—it targeted the software they trusted.

### 1. How It Happened (The Mechanism)

Instead of breaking into the final targets (like the US Treasury or Microsoft), hackers broke into **SolarWinds**, a company that provides IT management software called **Orion**.

- **Infiltration:** <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">Hackers gained access to SolarWinds’ internal systems</mark> (possibly through a weak password like "solarwinds123" or by compromising their build environment).
    
- **The "Trojan Horse":** They <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">injected malicious code into a legitimate software update for the Orion platform</mark>.
    
- **Distribution:** Because the update was "official" and digitally signed by SolarWinds, customers' security systems trusted it. <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">SolarWinds unknowingly sent this infected update</mark> to about **18,000 customers**.
    

---

### 2. The Timeline

- **Late 2019:** Hackers first gained access and began "test runs" to see if they could inject code without being caught.
    
- **March – June 2020:** The malicious updates were distributed to customers.
    
- **The "Dormant" Period:** The malware was designed to stay quiet for up to two weeks after installation to avoid detection by behavior-monitoring tools.
    
- **December 2020:** The cybersecurity firm **FireEye** discovered the breach after noticing their own internal tools had been stolen.
    

---

### 3. Who Was Affected?

The scale was unprecedented. While 18,000 organizations downloaded the update, the hackers only "activated" the second stage of the attack on a few hundred high-value targets for active spying.

- **US Government:** Departments of Homeland Security, Treasury, State, Commerce, and Energy (including the National Nuclear Security Administration).
    
- **Tech Giants:** Microsoft, Intel, Cisco, and NVIDIA.
    
- **Cybersecurity Firms:** FireEye (which eventually blew the whistle).
    

---

### 4. Who Was Responsible?

The US government and security experts attributed the attack to **APT29** (also known as "Cozy Bear"), a hacking group associated with the **Russian Foreign Intelligence Service (SVR)**. Their goal appeared to be long-term intelligence gathering rather than immediate destruction.

## How SolarWinds Attack Was Carried Out

The SolarWinds assault was a typical supply chain attack. In these kinds of hacks, the attackers don’t go after their victims’ networks directly. Instead, they penetrate the system of a third-party supplier with access to their targets’ network assets. In this case, the third-party supplier was SolarWinds.

Many of SolarWinds’ customers use a system called Orion, which is a performance monitoring solution that tracks the status of SolarWinds' Orion customers. It has privileged access to gather performance data and other information from logs generated by customer IT assets.

This made SolarWinds an ideal target for hackers who successfully gained access to several thousand companies' networks.
