## 🕵️‍♂️ The Hacking Story

A company owns a **valuable server** that stores customer data. This server is the **Asset**.  
Because it holds sensitive information, its **Asset Value** is very high.

One day, a hacker notices that the server software hasn’t been updated in years. That weakness is a **Vulnerability** — a flaw that _could_ be abused.

The hacker creates a clever technique to take advantage of that weakness. That technique is the **Exploit** — the _method_ used to break in.

Late at night, the hacker launches the **Attack** by running the exploit against the server.

Once inside, the hacker delivers malicious code that steals passwords and sends them away. That malicious code is the **Payload** — what actually does the damage.

Now the hacker asks:  
“How useful is this to me?”  
The answer is the **Hack Value** — maybe the data can be sold, used for blackmail, or used to access other systems.

But there’s a bigger picture.

The company realizes there are hackers actively looking for systems like theirs. That possibility is the **Threat** — the _potential_ for someone to cause harm.

When you combine:

- how serious the **Vulnerability** is
    
- how likely the **Threat** is
    
- and how valuable the **Asset** is
    

You get **Risk** — the chance that real damage will actually happen.
