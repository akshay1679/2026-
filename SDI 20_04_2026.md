## **Chain of Custody in Cyber Investigations**

- **Chain of Custody (CoC)** is the <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">chronological documented record of possession, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of evidence</mark>.
    
- It ensures that digital evidence remains:
    
    - authentic
        
    - unaltered
        
    - legally admissible in court
        
- CoC acts as the **legal backbone of digital evidence** in cyber investigations.
    
- Since digital evidence can be changed very easily, CoC is critical to prove integrity.
    
- A technically strong forensic investigation becomes useless if the Chain of Custody is broken.

## Importance of Chain of Custody

- Proves:
    
    - who handled the evidence
        
    - where it was stored
        
    - when it was transferred
        
    - why it was accessed
        
- Confirms that the evidence presented in court is the same as the one originally seized.
    
- Protects against:
    
    - tampering
        
    - contamination
        
    - substitution
        
    - legal rejection
        
- Uses:
    
    - <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">hash verification</mark>
        
    - <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;"> cryptographic signatures</mark>
        
    - <mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">tamper-evident seals</mark>
        

---

## Initiating the Chain (Collection and Seizure)

### 1. Unique Identification

- Assign:
    
    - case number
        
    - unique evidence ID
        
- Helps distinguish evidence from other items.
    

### 2. Physical Description

Record:

- make
    
- model
    
- serial number
    
- visible damage
    
- condition of the device
    

### 3. Seizure Information

Document:

- exact date
    
- time
    
- location
    
- name of collector
    
- signature of collector
    

### 4. Legal Authority

Mention basis of seizure:

- search warrant number
    
- consent form
    
- internal policy approval
    

### 5. Isolation and Sealing

- Place item in sealed tamper-evident bag/container
    
- Record unique seal number
    

### 6. Evidence Tagging

- Attach evidence label with:
    
    - evidence ID
        
    - short description
        

### 7. Initial Hash Calculation

- If live acquisition is done:
    
    - calculate and record hash immediately
         

---

## Documentation Protocols

### Standardized Forms

Use approved forms containing:

- item description
    
- seizure details
    
- transfer history
    
- final disposition
    

### Clear Signatures

Every transfer must include:

- giver’s signature
    
- receiver’s signature
    
- printed names
    
- date and time
    

### Purpose of Access

Mention reason for access such as:

- forensic imaging
    
- malware analysis
    
- court presentation
    

### Permanent Ink Rule

- Entries should be made in permanent ink
    
- No erasing allowed
    
- Corrections:
    
    - single line through error
        
    - initials beside correction
        

### Digital Backup

- Maintain secure digital copies of:
    
    - CoC form
        
    - hash reports
        
    - acquisition logs
        

### Minimal Gaps

- Time between seizure and secure storage should be minimal and documented
    

### Witness Verification

- In sensitive cases:
    
    - second investigator should co-sign
        

---

## Secure Storage and Evidence Handling

### Controlled Access Vault

- Evidence stored in secure locked vault
    
- Features:
    
    - limited access
        
    - video surveillance
        

### Environmental Control

Protect against:

- humidity
    
- temperature damage
    
- static electricity
    

Use:

- anti-static bags
    
- Faraday bags
    

### Logging System

- Separate vault access log must be maintained
    

### Original vs Working Copy

- Original evidence → securely archived
    
- Analysis done only on:
    
    - forensic image
        
    - working copy
        

This prevents accidental damage to original evidence.

---

## Transfer and Transport

- Transfers are high-risk points for CoC failure
    

### Rules During Transfer

- keep evidence sealed
    
- locked container required
    
- within physical possession of transporter
    
- maintain line of sight
    

### External Hand-Offs

Example:

- sending to external decryption lab
    

Requires:

- detailed receipt
    
- recipient verification
    
- recipient lab’s CoC standards check
    

---

## Analysis Phase and Internal Control

### Image Integrity Check

- Verify forensic image hash every time before analysis
    

### Dedicated Workstations

- Use isolated forensic systems
    
- Prevent write-back to evidence
    

### Examiner Access Log

Record:

- who accessed
    
- start time
    
- end time
    
- findings generated
    

### Non-Destructive Tools

- Use validated forensic software only
    

### Audit Trail Generation

Software should log:

- filters used
    
- keyword searches
    
- actions performed
    

### Findings Integrity

Extracted files must:

- be hashed immediately
    
- have separate CoC records
    

### Expert Witness Preparation

- Organize all CoC records for:
    
    - legal team review
        
    - courtroom cross-examination
        

---

## Consequences of CoC Failure (Spoliation)

### Spoliation

Means:

- destruction of evidence
    
- significant alteration
    
- improper handling creating doubt
    

### Results of CoC Failure

- evidence may become inadmissible
    
- case may collapse
    
- disciplinary action possible
    
- ethical review may occur
    
- loss of professional credibility
    

---

## Conclusion

- Chain of Custody is the **most important procedural requirement** in cyber investigations.
    
- It guarantees:
    
    - authenticity
        
    - integrity
        
    - accountability
        
- Proper CoC includes:
    
    - tamper seals
        
    - secure storage
        
    - transfer logs
        
    - hash verification
        
    - complete documentation
        
- Strong CoC protects against:
    
    - legal challenges
        
    - spoliation claims
        
- Without CoC, even strong digital evidence can be rejected.

## Quick FAQs Notes

### Primary purpose of CoC

- To prove evidence remained protected and unchanged
    

### What is a CoC break?

- Any undocumented gap in evidence handling
    

### Does CoC apply to forensic images?

- Yes, absolutely
    

### What is continuity of evidence?

- Proof of continuous verified control
    

### Why minimize transfers?

- Each transfer increases risk of error or contamination
    

### Why use tamper-evident seals?

- To show unauthorized opening
    

### Why use permanent ink?

- Prevents unauthorized changes
    

### Who starts CoC?

- First person who seizes the evidence
    

### When does CoC end?

- After final disposal or return of evidence

# 1. Evidence Integrity (Non-Repudiation)

## Meaning

Evidence integrity means proving that the digital evidence has **not been changed, modified, or tampered with** from the time it was collected until it is presented in court.

This supports **non-repudiation**, meaning nobody can deny the authenticity of the evidence.

---

## Steps to Maintain Evidence Integrity

### Step 1: Initial Hash of Original Evidence

Before touching the device, investigators calculate a **hash value** of the original evidence.

Example:

- Laptop hard disk
    
- USB drive
    
- Mobile phone storage
    

Common hashing algorithms:

- MD5
    
- SHA-1
    
- SHA-256 (preferred)
    

Example:  
Original Hash = `abc123xyz`

---

### Step 2: Disk Imaging (Bit-by-Bit Copy)

A complete forensic copy of the storage device is created.

This is called:

## Disk Imaging

It is:

- exact copy
    
- sector-by-sector copy
    
- bit-by-bit copy
    

It includes:

- deleted files
    
- hidden files
    
- slack space
    
- unallocated space
    
- metadata
    

Not just visible files.

---

### Step 3: Second Hash of the Image

After imaging, another hash is generated for the copied image.

If:

Initial Hash = Second Hash

then:

## Evidence is unchanged

This proves:

- no tampering
    
- no corruption
    
- valid forensic image
    

---

### Step 4: Work Only on the Image

Investigators must:

## NEVER work on original evidence

Always:

- preserve original evidence
    
- analyze only the copied image
    

This protects evidence from accidental modification.

# 2. Chain of Custody

---

## Meaning

Chain of custody is the complete record of:

- who handled the evidence
    
- when they handled it
    
- where they handled it
    
- how they handled it
    
- what action they performed
    

It ensures accountability.

---

## Why It Is Important

Without proper chain of custody:

- evidence may be rejected in court
    
- evidence can be challenged
    
- investigation becomes weak
    

---

## Information Recorded

Every person handling evidence must be recorded properly.

Required details:

### 1. Name

Who handled the evidence

### 2. Time

When they handled it

### 3. Location

Where the evidence was handled

### 4. Action Performed

What exactly they did

Examples:

- collected
    
- transported
    
- imaged
    
- analyzed
    
- stored
    

---

## Example Table

|Step|Person|Action|
|---|---|---|
|1|Investigator 1|Collected the laptop|
|2|Investigator 2|Created disk image|
|3|Investigator 3|Performed analysis on image|

### 2. Sealed

## Tamper-proof bags

# 4. Reporting

---

## Meaning

Final investigation findings must be documented properly.

This is called:

# Forensic Report

It must be:

- clear
    
- factual
    
- professional
    
- unbiased
    

---

## Report Should Include

---

### 1. Summary of Findings

Brief explanation of what was discovered

Example:  
Unauthorized SSH brute-force attack detected

---

### 2. Timeline of Events

Chronological order of events

Example:

- 10:00 AM — suspicious login attempts started
    
- 10:15 AM — privilege escalation occurred
    
- 10:30 AM — malicious script executed
    

---

### 3. Evidence Details

Include:

- device information
    
- hash values
    
- image details
    
- tools used
    
- logs collected
    

---

### 4. Conclusion Based on Facts

Only evidence-supported conclusion

Not assumptions

Example:

Correct:  
Logs show the user executed malicious commands

Wrong:  
User is definitely guilty

# 5. Ethics and Principles

---

# A. Not Biased

---

## Rule

Only facts should be present

Never:

- assumptions
    
- personal opinions
    
- emotional judgment
    

---

## Example

Wrong:  
“The suspect is clearly guilty”

Correct:  
“Logs show the user executed those actions”

---

# B. Repeatability

---

## Meaning

Another expert should be able to:

- follow the same process
    
- use same tools
    
- reach same results
    

This improves credibility.

---

# C. Minimal Evidence Modification

---

## Rule

Evidence should not be changed.

If modification is necessary:

## It must be documented

Example:

- system needed to be powered on
    
- volatile memory needed acquisition
    

Document:

- what changed
    
- why it changed
    
- who approved it
